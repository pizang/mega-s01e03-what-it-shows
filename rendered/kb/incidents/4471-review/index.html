The original incident page calls this one known parser issue with one fix queued. Its own computed view separates a shared-parser problem from a booking-form hint problem. Those need separate engineering tickets and separate fixes. This review records evidence from the export dated 4 October 2026; it does not claim that either fix has shipped or that the export is the current queue.

The follow-up engineering tickets are the shared parser and the booking-v3 hint. The table below describes filing in the exported desk snapshot, before these GitHub tickets were created.

Parser: the shared parser assumes month first

For day-first tenants, the shared parser interprets an intended day/month date as month/day. App and email reports remain in this group even when a tenant runs booking-v3; its form hint is not involved in those channels. Booking-v2 form reports also fall here.

Evidence: R-41200 describes 7 April becoming 4 July for harbour.dental (en-GB, booking-v2, app). R-41203 describes 2 December becoming 12 February for lantern.row (cs-CZ, booking-v2, app). R-41205 belongs here despite its intake hint tag and en-US browser locale: morrow.tax is a fr-FR tenant and this is an app report.

Engineering should make date parsing respect the tenant’s date order, then check both ambiguous day/month inputs and unambiguous dates in affected channels. This is an inference from the kb’s classifier and reports, not a reproduction against product code: the product implementation is not included in this export.

Hint: booking-v3 tells day-first users to type month first

Booking-v3 displays MM/DD/YYYY, but the form interprets the entry in the tenant’s day-first order. This distinct path affects form reports from alder.physio (de-DE), copper.studio (es-ES), and pale.harbour (pt-PT). A shared-parser fix alone does not correct that instruction.

Evidence: R-41230 is a form report from copper.studio describing 6 January becoming 1 June, although intake tagged it parser. R-41241 is a form report from alder.physio with an en-US browser locale; the tenant remains de-DE. R-41251 is a pale.harbour form report describing 7 January becoming 1 July and occurs twice in the overlapping export.

Engineering should align the displayed hint with the tenant-specific interpretation and check the displayed instruction and resulting date together for all affected tenants. This mechanism comes from DATE_HINT and causeOf; the export does not include a live booking form to exercise.

Reports that the classification does not explain fully

desk-notes.json flags R-41202, R-41209, R-41236, R-41344, and R-41620 as read as a one-day move. Their descriptions include adjacent-day dates, a June-only offset, an invite arriving before booking, and different days in Lisbon and Toronto. The classifier places all of them under parser by tenant and channel; the computed footnote above keeps them in that count. This does not establish that a month/day parser explains those symptoms.

Keep those IDs visible on the parser ticket and ask for original timestamps, time zones, and reproduction details before deciding whether they warrant another ticket. Do not silently drop them, double-count them, or assert a time-zone root cause from these words alone.

The queue kept receiving reports

The chart uses received and merged timestamps separately. The merge spike records desk activity, not the start of the bug. Reports arrived before the mass merge and continued through the end of the export. A known status, a queued fix, and a large merged ticket are not evidence that the incoming problem stopped.

Triage by cause, not by subject

Decision 0001 and the triage page prescribe subject-only merging. Both were last reviewed in June 2024; the shared layout marks them stale. An accepted historical decision is not proof that it still matches the executable behavior. Leave that accepted record intact; a new decision should replace it rather than rewriting its history.

The explorer runs the exported rules. Different subjects can describe the same cause. Matching subjects can hide different causes, including within one tenant across channels, or between tenants on the same channel. Unknown tenants do not establish a shared cause.

Proposed replacement rule: identify the tenant, its locale, the form version, and the report's channel; inspect the body and compare causes. Merge only when the cause is established to be the same. If the tenant or cause is unknown, keep the reports separate until investigated. A subject match is a search aid, not a merge decision.

Split the hint reports out of #4471, retain the parser reports with their anomaly notes, and link the two engineering issues. Recheck the live queue before applying these snapshot counts. The original accepted decision and triage page still need an explicit replacement by the desk; this review does not pretend that publishing a page changes its automation.
